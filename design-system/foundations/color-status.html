Status

Three outcomes with fixed meanings: a form that saved, an input that will cause trouble, an operation that failed. Same three roles per token as the categorical scale.

Never map a status onto --data-n, or the reverse. Numbered slots are reassignable by design; meanings are not. Point --danger at --data-1 and error becomes re-themeable to whatever hue slot 1 happens to be next year — an error state that quietly stops looking like one.

Louder than categorical, on purpose

Danger sits at hue 27 against --data-1 at 25; success at 150 against --data-4 at 150. The collision is deliberate — conventional hues are too deeply learned to renumber for internal tidiness. They are separated by chroma instead: every status token is authored at higher chroma than the categorical token in the same role, and the verifier fails if that stops being true.

Category--data-1-* · hue 25
Danger--danger-* · hue 27
Category--data-4-* · hue 150
Success--success-* · hue 150

A warning should out-shout a category: grabbing attention is its whole job, where a categorical slot's job is to sit quietly and be equal to its peers.

There is no --info

Informational messaging is the brand accent — use --accent and --surface-card. A fourth status meaning "nothing has gone wrong" invites consumers to reach for it as a second accent.

Status always carries text, usually an icon. An error indicated only by a red border is invisible to a colorblind user and to anyone who has not learned the convention. This is the layer where that rule matters most.
